Why you feel so bad:

Your feelings are right.

You live in a world that isn't built for humans.

You live among strangers and work for people who would replace you in a week. No human was built for that, and your body keeps telling you so. Doctors, coaches and apps each sell you a different fault to fix in yourself. Unless a test finds a physical cause, there is nothing in you to fix.

“I'm depressed.”

A normal reaction to a world that isn't built for humans.

Everyone feels low when their people are gone, they lose respect, or effort gets them nowhere. Today all three are how most people live.

What really is: Depression

“I'm lonely.”

You don't have enough people.

Humans lived in camps of a few dozen, with about five people they could fully count on. Count yours. Followers don't count.

The science

“I dread going to work.”

You leave your people to work for strangers.

Humans used to work next to the people they lived with. Now you leave them every morning and spend the day with people you barely know. Of course you dread it.

The science

“I'm burned out.”

Giving up on work that never ends.

Your body goes passive when effort stops showing a result. Your task list refills every morning. A bath doesn't shorten it.

What really is: BurnoutThe science

“My life has no point.”

Nobody you know needs you.

Purpose is the feeling of being needed by people you know. You work for strangers, so the feeling fades. A life coach will tell you it's inside you, at $60 to $200 an hour.

What really is: Purpose

“I'm awkward around people.”

Being careful around strangers.

You were built for a life where you knew nearly everyone. Now you deal with strangers all day. Shyness wears off once people know you. Nobody makes money from that.

What really is: ShynessThe science

“I feel like a fraud.”

Nobody here has seen your work yet.

Your standing used to rest on what the people around you had watched you do for years. In a new job nobody has watched anything. You feel unproven because you are. It passes as they see you work.

What really is: Imposter syndrome

“Everyone is doing better than me.”

You're comparing yourself with everyone alive.

Humans want to be the best at something. For most of human history that meant the best among a few dozen people who knew you. Now a screen shows you the best on Earth at everything, every day. Nobody wins that.

The science

“I hate my boss.”

Humans had no bosses.

In a hunter-gatherer camp nobody could give another adult orders. Anyone who tried was mocked, ignored or thrown out. Now someone tells you when to arrive, when to eat and when you may go home.

The science

“I can't cope with my kids.”

A camp's job on one or two adults.

Raising a child took a whole camp: grandmothers, aunts, older kids and other mothers. You do it with one other tired adult, or alone. It is too much for two people.

What really is: Parental burnoutThe science

“I can't listen to my baby cry.”

No baby slept alone.

In a study of 173 societies, babies slept in the same room as their mother in every one. None gave the baby a room of its own. Wanting to go in is completely normal.

The science

“I can't focus.”

Restless people, made to sit still.

ADHD is a name for people who move a lot and notice everything, once school or an office puts them in a chair all day.

What really is: ADHD

“I'm restless.”

You sit all day.

Your body was built to be on its feet most of the day, walking, carrying and digging. An hour at the gym is one hour. You sit through the rest.

The science

“I can't sleep.”

Lights on until midnight.

Your body sets its clock by darkness, and for 300,000 years the evening was dark. In a sleep lab, people who read on a lit screen before bed had their body clock pushed back by more than an hour and a half.

The science

“I'm anxious all the time.”

Danger you can't fight or run from.

Fear is built for danger that is in front of you and over in minutes. You can't fight a war on another continent, and you can't outrun a bill. So the fear stays on.

The science

“I can't stop scrolling.”

People-watching.

Humans are built to watch other humans, and the feed is an endless supply of people who will never know you. Not being able to stop is completely normal.

What really is: Short form video addiction

“I watch too much porn.”

Fake sex partners.

Your body reacts to them as if they were real. A new one appears at every click. None of them will ever meet you.

What really is: Porn

“I can't stop eating.”

Food built so you can't stop.

Food companies adjust the sugar, fat and salt in a product until people eat as much of it as possible. You failing to stop is the result they tested for.

So now what

  • Ask a doctor to check for a physical cause, such as a thyroid fault.
  • See the same people in the same place every week.
  • Write down who needed you this week.
  • Show finished work to someone you know.
  • Get the same people on fixed days: a grandparent, neighbour or friend who takes the kids at the same time every week.
  • Fill the evening first. A blocked app leaves the same empty hours.

Humans need a world for humans.
